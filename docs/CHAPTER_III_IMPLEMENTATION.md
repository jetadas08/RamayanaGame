# Chapter III — Across the Ocean

## Gameplay identity

Chapter III teaches one evolving skill: **read the obstacle before choosing the response**. Its loop is evidence → interpretation → commitment → consequence → repair → canonical resolution. This differs from Chapter I's observation and alliance work and Chapter II's search-board evidence classification.

The persistent **Crossing Trail** records the obstacle, response, and working principle for the five encounters. It is a concise chapter reasoning aid; the separate character Memory Trail stores the longer-term meaning learned about Hanumān.

## Systems and compatibility

- Chapter III uses a dedicated chapter map with a continuous narrative ocean route. Maināka, Surasā, and Siṃhikā remain route events rather than precise modern geographic points.
- The map explicitly separates Hanumān's crossing from the later Rāma Setu route.
- Crossing Trail answers persist locally and through account progress, merge without overwriting completed reasoning, and round-trip through the current schema.
- Existing mastery IDs remain stable. Legacy correct responses are recognized alongside the new activity answers so old stars and completed stages survive the redesign.
- Wrong answers remain nonblocking; correct retakes preserve the best score.
- Discover tests recognition, Understand tests explanation, and Go Deeper tests transfer. The new Go Deeper activities do not repeat immediate recall.
- Radio controls, fieldsets, legends, status announcements, keyboard-native inputs, responsive one-column layouts, and reduced dependence on color preserve the existing accessibility foundation.

## Comparison

| Dimension | Chapter I | Chapter II | Chapter III |
|---|---|---|---|

| Agency | Observe people and form interpretations. | Classify evidence and assemble a search plan. | Commit, assess, adapt, diagnose, and interpret. |
| Consequence | Dialogue and alliance meaning become visible. | Evidence updates a persistent board. | Each choice produces targeted feedback and updates the Crossing Trail. |
| Interaction variety | Hotspots, observation, dialogue, connection work. | Evidence classification, testimony, route reasoning. | Mission assembly, ambiguous assessment, two-step revision, comparison, synthesis. |
| Mastery depth | Recognition of people and relational meaning. | Explanation of evidence and planning logic. | Recognition → explanation → transfer of response principles. |
| Skill progression | Observation becomes understanding and alliance. | Testimony becomes direction and responsibility. | Known preparation → ambiguity → revision → comparison → synthesis. |
| Replay value | Revisit observations and mastery. | Reconstruct evidence and search logic. | Retake transfer tasks and compare obstacle principles; authored Explore choices remain stable rather than randomized. |

## Remaining weaknesses

- Chapter III continues to use the existing scene paintings. The interaction design now varies, but the visual consequence of each response is primarily textual rather than animated in the art.
- Explore encounters are authored, deterministic cases. Replay benefits from mastery retakes and cross-encounter comparison, but Explore does not yet provide randomized variants.
- The Crossing Trail records the chosen resolution; it does not preserve a full history of abandoned hypotheses.
